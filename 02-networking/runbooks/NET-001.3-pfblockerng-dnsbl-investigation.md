## ##Objective

Explain the session's Entertainment VLAN traffic findings and provide a workflow for investigating traffic involving the DNSBL sinkhole.

**Next Logical Step:** Review the recorded sinkhole configuration before classifying captured traffic, because DNSBL can account for a local destination address.

## ##Recorded Configuration and Observations

| Item | Session finding |
|---|---|
| Affected client | Samsung TV, 192.168.20.100, Entertainment VLAN 20 |
| Entertainment gateway | 192.168.20.1 |
| pfBlockerNG DNSBL | Active |
| IPv4 DNSBL sinkhole | 10.10.10.1 |
| Address association | pfSense LAN IP alias with /32 mask |
| Domains involved in observed DNSBL behavior | log-ingestion.samsungacr.com; pagead2.googlesyndication.com |
| TV operation | Streaming remained functional during the observed period |

Packet captures and Wireshark analysis associated traffic involving `10.10.10.1` with pfBlockerNG DNSBL. The TV generated DNS and telemetry-related requests involving Samsung logging and advertising services.

The investigation established that DNSBL can redirect blocked DNS requests to a local sinkhole instead of returning the original public destination address. A capture involving `10.10.10.1` should therefore be examined alongside DNSBL logs and resolver behavior before being classified as an unknown host or malicious activity.

**Next Logical Step:** Correlate the observed domains with DNSBL logs and resolver behavior, because the sinkhole address alone does not explain every captured flow.

## ##Investigation Workflow — For Future Use

1. Identify the client, current address, VLAN and observed domain or destination.
2. Review the current DNSBL sinkhole configuration and associated IP alias. The address above reflects this session's configuration.
3. Compare DNSBL log entries and resolver responses with the relevant client traffic.
4. Capture the interface carrying that traffic and examine it in Wireshark.
5. Record the domain, returned address, client behavior and correlated log or capture reference.
6. Test the intended client service and document its result separately from DNSBL findings.
7. Preserve representative redacted evidence before proposing any policy change.

This workflow is proposed for future use. No new captures, log collection, allowlist changes or DNSBL changes were performed during documentation generation.

**Next Logical Step:** Preserve representative redacted logs and captures during a future investigation, because repeatable diagnosis requires correlated evidence.

## ##Verification and Evidence Limits

Samsung TV traffic on VLAN 20 and DNSBL-related behavior were observed. TV streaming remained functional during the investigation. Complete application and service testing was not performed.

The handoff supplies no raw log entries, screenshots or capture artifacts. Do not interpret the observations as proof that every TV service works, that all advertising traffic is blocked, or that Entertainment isolation is validated.

**Next Logical Step:** Test the intended TV services and review Entertainment access policy, because streaming during the session did not validate every application or isolation rule.
